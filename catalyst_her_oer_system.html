01 · SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

Six components. One cooperative catalyst.

Traditional catalysts optimize a single reaction. This composite strategically layers six functional materials — each solving a distinct limitation — so their combined behavior exceeds what any one part could do alone. This is the same architecture powering the HELIX-D deployment framework and NEXUS-H fleet layer.

Cathode · Hydrogen Engine

MAC-FeMoS₂

Iron-doped molybdenum disulfide encapsulated in amorphous carbon. Fe-doping tunes the S edge-site binding energy toward the thermoneutral point for H* adsorption, driving the Hydrogen Evolution Reaction (HER) with low overpotential.

Drives HER
Anode · Oxygen Generator

NiSe₂

Nickel diselenide provides the active sites for the four-electron Oxygen Evolution Reaction (OER), the kinetically sluggish half of water splitting, converting OH⁻ to O₂ and closing the electron circuit.

Drives OER
Substrate · Electron Highway

Boron-Doped Diamond (BDD)

A conductive, corrosion-resistant scaffold that anchors both catalytic layers. Boron doping gives diamond semiconducting behavior, enabling ultrafast electron transfer while resisting the corrosive conditions of seawater electrolysis.

Structural + Conductive
Capture Layer

ZIF-8

A zeolitic imidazolate framework with high surface area and CO₂-selective micropores. It concentrates dilute CO₂ from mixed gas streams directly at the catalytic site, feeding the carbon valorization loop.

Selective CO₂ Capture
Reduction Initiator

Copper (Cu)

Initiates CO₂ reduction and uniquely enables C–C coupling, the step that builds multi-carbon products (ethylene, ethanol) rather than stopping at single-carbon CO.

CO₂ → C–C Bonds
Hydrogenation Finisher

Ruthenium (Ru)

Drives hydrogenation of reduction intermediates toward methanol and long-chain hydrocarbons via Sabatier/Fischer-Tropsch-type pathways, using H₂ generated on-site.

Finishes Fuel Synthesis
Emergent property: Because HER, OER, CO₂ capture, and CO₂ conversion are spatially organized on one conductive scaffold, the reactor runs water splitting and carbon utilization concurrently — turning an electrolyzer into a combined hydrogen + chemical factory instead of two separate plants.
02 · THE ELECTRICITY-FROM-WATER ENGINE

How HER and OER actually generate usable hydrogen fuel

Electrical current, supplied by renewable generation, drives two coupled half-reactions at opposite electrodes. Splitting the labor across MAC-FeMoS₂ and NiSe₂ is what lets the system hit low overpotentials on both sides at once — the main efficiency bottleneck in conventional electrolyzers.

In practice the applied cell voltage exceeds the 1.23 V thermodynamic minimum because of activation, ohmic, and mass-transport overpotentials on each electrode. The purpose of MAC-FeMoS₂ and NiSe₂ is to push both half-reactions' activation overpotentials down simultaneously, which is what keeps total electrical input — and therefore the cost per kilogram of H₂ — low.

Cathode — MAC-FeMoS₂ on BDD

Hydrogen Evolution Reaction (HER)
  • Fe-doping shifts the Mo-edge ΔG(H*) toward zero, the ideal binding energy for the Sabatier volcano peak.
  • Amorphous carbon encapsulation increases active edge-site density and prevents sulfur leaching / active-site poisoning.
  • BDD substrate supplies a low-resistance, corrosion-immune electron pathway even in chloride-rich seawater.
  • Net effect: low HER overpotential sustained over long operating windows.

Anode — NiSe₂ on BDD

Oxygen Evolution Reaction (OER)
  • Se substitution modifies Ni's d-band center, stabilizing the OOH* intermediate that is normally the OER's rate-limiting step.
  • Operates without generating chlorine gas in seawater, avoiding the corrosive Cl₂/hypochlorite byproducts that destroy conventional anodes.
  • BDD anchoring resists the highly oxidizing anodic environment where most catalyst supports degrade fastest.
  • Net effect: the four-electron OER — normally the main efficiency bottleneck — proceeds at competitive current density.

03 · DEPLOYMENT ENVIRONMENTS

Built to run where the water actually is

Conventional electrolyzers require purified freshwater. Because BDD resists corrosion and NiSe₂ avoids chlorine evolution, this catalyst runs across freshwater, seawater, and atmospheric-moisture-harvested water — unlocking deployment in coastal, arid, and remote settings.

Freshwater

Baseline operating condition. Lowest ionic resistance, highest efficiency reference point for the system.

Reference case

Seawater

NiSe₂'s selectivity suppresses competing chlorine evolution at the anode, while BDD resists chloride-induced corrosion — enabling direct seawater splitting without pre-desalination.

No chlorine byproduct

Atmospheric Moisture

Coupled with atmospheric water harvesting, the reactor can be supplied in arid or remote regions with no local liquid water source.

Off-grid capable
Deployment implication: Removing the freshwater constraint expands the theoretical hydrogen-production footprint to roughly 71% of Earth's surface — the fraction covered by ocean, coastline, and moisture-bearing atmosphere.
04 · CARBON LOOP CLOSURE

CO₂ as feedstock, not waste

ZIF-8 concentrates dilute CO₂ at the catalytic surface; Cu initiates reduction and C–C coupling; Ru drives hydrogenation to finished fuels. This runs in parallel with HER/OER, so the same reactor that makes hydrogen also converts captured carbon into sellable chemicals.

Representative valorization pathway
CO₂ + 3H₂ → CH₃OH + H₂O
Cu + Ru
Economic reframe: ZIF-8 capture + Cu/Ru conversion turns CO₂ handling from a cost center (sequestration) into a revenue stream (methanol, formate, hydrocarbons) — while consuming H₂ produced in the same reactor.

06 · IMPLICATIONS

From lab chemistry to civilization-scale infrastructure

The thesis argues that interface engineering, electronic coupling, and spatial organization of otherwise-incompatible reactions enable cooperative catalysis unreachable by single-phase materials — with direct downstream impact on energy, industry, climate, and water systems.

Technological Impact

Societal Impact

Addresses energy security, climate change, and water scarcity simultaneously — without forcing tradeoffs between economic growth and environmental protection.
