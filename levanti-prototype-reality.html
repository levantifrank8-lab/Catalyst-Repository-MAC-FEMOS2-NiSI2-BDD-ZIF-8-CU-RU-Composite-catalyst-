Where the game is a simplification

The game abstracted six real material-science literatures into single "level" numbers and let you sell instantly into a liquid market. None of that is true yet in reality. The table below is the honest translation — what each game system stands for, and what actually has to happen for it to become real.

Game mechanic → real-world equivalent

The honest gap: Iron-doped MoS₂ (HER), NiSe₂ (OER), boron-doped diamond electrodes, ZIF-8 CO₂ capture, and Cu/Ru CO₂ reduction to hydrocarbons are each independently active research areas with published literature. The specific claim in the thesis — that all six can be co-deposited into one monolithic composite electrode and operate together across freshwater, seawater, and atmospheric-moisture modes at industrial scale — has not been experimentally demonstrated as a single integrated system. That integration, not any one component, is the real R&D risk and the first thing a prototype program has to test.

Bench electrode calculator (Faraday's law)

This is real electrochemistry, not a game formula: mass produced = (current × time × molar mass) ÷ (electrons transferred × Faraday's constant). Use it to size a lab test cell for a target output.

Reference points: a typical PEM/alkaline lab test cell runs 1–25 cm² at 0.2–1.0 A/cm²; a pilot stack scales to 100s–1,000s of cm² per cell repeated across dozens of cells.

Levelized cost of hydrogen (LCOH) calculator

Standard techno-economic-analysis formula used across the electrolyzer industry: LCOH = (annualized CAPEX + annual OPEX) ÷ annual kg H₂ produced. Adjust the inputs to see how your prototype's assumptions compare to today's real market range.
